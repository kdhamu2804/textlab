Slide 2 of 8

The problem

Many people write about stress online before they ask anyone for help, and moderators cannot read every post. A tool that flags stressed posts can help humans notice them first.

Our research question

Can a fine-tuned RoBERTa model find stress in Reddit posts better than a simple word-counting model?

Speaker notes: Why did we choose this? Many people write about their worries online before they talk to anyone, and on a big site like Reddit moderators cannot read every post. A tool that flags posts where the writer sounds stressed can help a human look at them first. It only says how a post sounds; it is not a medical diagnosis. Our research question: can a fine-tuned RoBERTa model find stress better than a simple word-counting model called TF-IDF? We set two goals before starting: beat TF-IDF by at least 3 F1 points, and still work on topics the model never saw. Everything is measured on 715 test posts the models never saw while learning.

Speaker notes: We use two datasets. The main one is Dreaddit, made at Columbia University in 2019: Reddit posts from 10 communities, labelled 1 for stress or 0 for no stress by human annotators. It has 2,838 posts to train on and 715 to test on. The second is GoEmotions from Google, used only for our extension: short Reddit comments with 27 emotions plus neutral. The coloured bar shows how we use Dreaddit: 2,270 posts to learn from, 568 to check settings, called validation, and 715 test posts that stay hidden until the very end, like a secret exam paper. Both datasets are free on Hugging Face and are in our data folder.

Slide 5 of 8

The models

Three models, from simple to smart

1

Majority class

Role: the floor

Ignores the text and always says “stress”, the most common label.

Why: any real model must beat it, or it learned nothing.

2

TF-IDF + logistic regression

Role: the baseline

Counts important words and adds up a learned weight for each one.

3

RoBERTa (fine-tuned)

Role: our main model

A language model that already knows English. We train it a little more on our posts.

Strength: reads the whole post and understands context.

Speaker notes: We compare three models. Model 1, majority class, ignores the text and always says stress. It is the floor: any real model must beat it. Model 2, TF-IDF with logistic regression, is our baseline. It counts important words and gives each word a weight. It is fast and simple, but it does not understand word order, so “not scared anymore” looks like “scared”. Model 3 is RoBERTa, our main model. It already learned English from a huge amount of text, and we fine-tune it on our posts. It reads the whole post and understands context.

Speaker notes: This slide shows how good our model is, in the simplest way. We tested each model on 715 posts it had never seen, and checked every answer against what people said. Green dots are posts it got right. Our AI model, RoBERTa, gets about 8 out of 10 right. A simple model that just counts words gets about 7 out of 10. Just guessing “stress” every time gets 5 out of 10. So our model makes about a third fewer mistakes than the simple model: 130 instead of 198. If someone asks for the technical score: RoBERTa’s F1 is 0.834 against 0.753 for TF-IDF, 8.1 points better, and our target was 3. We also checked it is not luck: we re-tested 10,000 times and it won every time, and it still works on topics it never saw.

Speaker notes: As an extra, we trained a second AI model that finds 27 emotions, like joy, fear and anger, and one post can have more than one. For example, for “I can’t stop thinking about the exam tomorrow. I’m scared I will fail.” it found fear, 80 percent, and nervousness, 16 percent. Common emotions like gratitude, love and joy are easy for it; rare ones like grief are still hard because there are very few examples. On the right is how anyone uses our website: paste a post on the Analyze page, click Analyze, and the Results page shows whether it sounds stressed, how sure the model is, which sentences mattered and which emotions it found. It answers in under a second, in about 84 milliseconds, and nothing you type is saved. Technical numbers if asked: emotions macro F1 is 0.509 for RoBERTa against 0.451 for TF-IDF. The emotions part runs in our laptop version; the free online server has too little memory for a second model.

Speaker notes: To sum up in one sentence: fine-tuned RoBERTa finds stress with an F1 of 0.834, 8.1 points better than the TF-IDF baseline, and it still works on topics it never saw. The win is statistically real, the model runs in a live web app, and as an extension we also detect 27 emotions. We are also honest about the limits: it is not a medical diagnosis; in our label check we disagreed with the dataset on 13 of 50 posts; the data is only English Reddit; and the model sees one post without the thread. Future work: more context, a mental-health model like MentalBERT, and other websites. Thank you, and we are happy to take questions.
